Everyday texts

What to reply to "how was your day?"

What it usually means: a friendly check-in, and often an open door to keep talking.

How to tell: If they ask it out of nowhere, or follow up on something you mentioned earlier, they want a real conversation. If it's just bounced back after you asked first, it's politeness.

Them
how was your day?
What to reply
Long, but the coffee was elite. How was yours?
Honestly a 6/10, saved by a good lunch. You?
Better now. Tell me one good thing that happened to you today.

Don't: answer "good" and stop, or send a full report of your day.

What to reply to "I'm tired"

What it usually means: they're low on energy, for the day or for this conversation.

How to tell: "I'm tired" plus a question back means they still want company, just low-effort. A one-line "I'm tired" late at night usually means they're wrapping up.

Them
I'm tired
What to reply
Sounds like a couch-and-snacks night. Go rest, talk tomorrow?
Long day? Want to vent, or want a distraction?
Fair. Sleep well, I'll text you tomorrow.

Don't: push for a long chat, or take it personally.

What to reply to "we'll see"

What it usually means: a soft maybe. Without anything else attached, it usually leans toward no.

How to tell: "We'll see!" with details or excitement ("depends on work, but I want to") is a real maybe. A flat "we'll see" after an invite is a polite hedge.

Them
we'll see
What to reply
Fair. I'll keep Saturday open, just let me know.
No pressure. If it works, it works.

Don't: ask for a definite answer, or ask again the same day.

Texts from a crush: what she means

What to reply to "haha"

What it usually means: she read it and liked it, but didn't give you anything to work with.

How to tell: If it's "haha" plus something else (a question, an emoji, a comeback), she's engaged. If "haha" is the whole reply to your question, she has nothing to add, and asking another question will feel like an interview.

Her
haha
What to reply
Okay, new topic: best thing you ate this week?
I'll take that as you agreeing with me
Alright, I owe you a better joke in person. Coffee Thursday?

Don't: send "haha" back, or apologize for the joke.

What to reply to "maybe another time"

What it usually means: either she's genuinely busy, or it's a polite no.

How to tell: A busy person suggests the other time ("maybe next week?"). A polite no doesn't. Neither one is a reason to push.

Her
maybe another time
What to reply
No worries. What does your next week look like?
Sounds good, you pick the day next time

Don't: ask "why?", guilt-trip, or keep inviting. If it's "maybe" twice with no other time offered, let it go.

What to reply to "still figuring it out"

What it usually means: her plans are open, and she may be leaving space for you to suggest something.

How to tell: If she said it after you asked "what are you up to this weekend", it's an opening. If she adds details ("might see friends"), she's telling you she's booked.

Her
still figuring it out
What to reply
Then I'll plan it. Saturday afternoon, you just show up.
Figuring it out is the fun part. Want a hint?
Let me guess: brunch, then pretending you'll be productive

Don't: ask "what do you want to do?". She just told you she doesn't know. Lead.

What to reply to "lol"

What it usually means: polite acknowledgement. Lowercase "lol" alone is the text version of a small smile.

How to tell: Same rule as "haha": look at what comes with it. "LOL" in capitals or "lmaooo" is a real laugh.

Her
lol
What to reply
Glad I could get a polite lol out of you. Next goal: a real one
Anyway, how did the thing you mentioned go?

Don't: explain the joke.

What to reply to "I'm so busy this week"

What it usually means: she's busy, and it says nothing either way about interest until you see what comes next.

How to tell: Interested and busy sounds like "this week is crazy but next week works". Not interested sounds like the busy message, every week.

Her
I'm so busy this week
What to reply
Sounds intense. Good luck with it. Let's do something next week when it calms down?
Go crush it. I'll check back Sunday

Don't: text every day "just checking in". Give her the week.

FAQ

What should I reply when someone asks "how was your day"?

Give one real detail and hand it back: "Long, but lunch was great. How was yours?" A one-word "good" ends the conversation; a full report is too much.

What to reply when someone says "I'm tired"?

Match their energy and give them an easy out: "Long day? Go rest, talk tomorrow." Don't push for a long chat.

What does "we'll see" mean?

Usually a soft maybe that leans no. If they add details or enthusiasm, it's a real maybe. Leave it open and don't ask for a definite answer.

What does it mean when she replies "haha"?

It depends on what came before. After a joke, it's usually genuine. As the whole reply to a question, it often means she has nothing to add. Change direction or suggest a plan instead of asking another question.

What does "maybe another time" mean?

Either she's busy or she's politely saying no. If she offers another time, she's busy. If she doesn't, ask once more lightly; if it's "maybe" again, let it go.

How do I know if someone is interested over text?

Look at effort, not words: do they ask questions back, add new topics, and agree to specific plans? One dry message means little; a pattern over several days means a lot.
